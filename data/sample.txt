Tokenizers chop text into pieces a model can count. Words are not the unit.
Letters are not the unit either. The unit is a learned piece — a token.

Byte-pair encoding (BPE) starts from the smallest units and greedily merges
the pairs it sees most often. Frequency buys short tokens. Rare spellings
stay long. That is why a common English word can be one token while your
name might be four.

low low low low low
lower lower
newest newest newest

Train on text. Count every adjacent pair. Merge the winner. Record the merge.
Replay those merges to encode. Paste the symbols back to decode. That is the
whole algorithm — five lines of idea, not a library.

Why teach BPE by hand? Because once you have merged low ×5, lower ×2, and
newest ×3 on paper, the mystery of "1,000 tokens is not 1,000 words" goes
away. Compression is visible. Ties go to the pair seen first so two honest
implementations match.

Keep the machine small. Pure Python. Standard library at runtime. No network
on the happy path. Comments written for a reader, not a benchmark.
